1.1 What is OpenEdge :link(intro_1),h4

OpenEdge is a kinetic Monte Carlo code for the edge of magnetically
confined plasmas and for plasma-material interaction.  It follows test
particles, meaning neutral atoms and molecules, impurity ions, and
solid or liquid particulates, through a plasma and magnetic-field
background that is prescribed rather than solved for.  The background
comes from an edge fluid code such as SOLPS-ITER, SOLEDGE3X, OEDGE or
EMC3-EIRENE, or from analytic or constant fields for verification.

Charged particles are advanced with a Boris integrator or, where the
gyro-orbit is small compared with the field scale, a guiding-center
integrator, with an automatic hand-off between the two.  Atomic
processes use ADAS rate coefficients.  Particles feel Coulomb drag,
thermal forces, cross-field diffusion and the wall sheath.  At the
wall they are reflected, sputter material, recycle, or are retained,
and the surface composition evolves in response.  Dust grains and
droplets carry their own size, mass, charge and temperature and are
heated, charged, dragged and evaporated by the plasma.

OpenEdge is built on "SPARTA"_sparta, the Direct Simulation Monte Carlo
code from Sandia National Laboratories, and inherits its hierarchical
Cartesian grid, cut-cell surface representation, particle storage, MPI
parallelism and Kokkos performance-portability layer.  Every SPARTA
input command remains available.  OpenEdge adds the physics commands
documented in this manual.

OpenEdge runs on single-processor laptops and on parallel machines
that compile C++17 and support "MPI"_mpi.  A Kokkos build runs on
multi-core CPUs and NVIDIA GPUs.

:link(mpi,https://www.mpi-forum.org)

This section highlights OpenEdge features, with links to specific

1.3 Grids, surfaces and plasma backgrounds :link(intro_3),h4

OpenEdge uses the SPARTA hierarchical Cartesian grid to store and sort
particles, in 3d, 2d or 2d axisymmetric geometry.  The vessel wall and
any internal components are triangulated surfaces, or line segments in
2d, that cut through grid cells.  Particles collide with these
surfaces and the surface models decide what happens.

The plasma background is separate from the particle grid.  It lives
on the native mesh of the edge code it came from: a quadrilateral B2
mesh and its EIRENE triangulation for SOLPS, a triangulated mesh for
SOLEDGE3X, or curvilinear zones for EMC3-EIRENE.  Each particle
samples the background at its own position through "fix
background"_fix_background.html, so the particle grid can be as coarse
or as refined as the transport problem needs without touching the
plasma data.

The magnetic field comes either from the poloidal flux map of the
equilibrium, which gives the field and its derivatives analytically,
or from field components stored with the background.  A separate
"processes.h5"_Section_howto.html#howto_6 database supplies atomic
rates and surface-interaction data.

1.4 Open source distribution :link(intro_4),h4

OpenEdge is distributed under the terms of the GNU General Public
License version 2, the license of the SPARTA code it is built on.  The
LICENSE and LICENSE.SPARTA files in the distribution give the full
text and the attribution of the SPARTA components.  The NOTICES file
lists third-party data and code included with OpenEdge and their
licenses.

The GPL means you can use, modify and redistribute OpenEdge, and that
any distributed derivative must also be released under the GPL.  If
you improve OpenEdge, please contribute the changes back through the
"repository"_ows so that others benefit.

1.5 Acknowledgments and citations :link(intro_5),h4

OpenEdge is developed at Oak Ridge National Laboratory.  It would not
exist without SPARTA, developed by Steve Plimpton and Michael Gallis
at Sandia National Laboratories.  The OpenEdge documentation reuses
the SPARTA manual for the commands that OpenEdge inherits.

Atomic data come from the "OPEN-ADAS"_adas project.  Sputter yield and
reflection tables are generated with the "RustBCA"_rustbca binary
collision code.  Plasma backgrounds are produced with SOLPS-ITER,
SOLEDGE3X, OEDGE and EMC3-EIRENE.

Citation information for OpenEdge will be added here when the
reference publications are available.  Please also cite SPARTA:

S. J. Plimpton, S. G. Moore, A. Borner, A. K. Stagg, T. P. Koehler,
J. R. Torczynski and M. A. Gallis, Direct Simulation Monte Carlo on
petaflop supercomputers and beyond, Physics of Fluids 31, 086101
(2019).
