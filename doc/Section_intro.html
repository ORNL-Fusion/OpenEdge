1.1 What is OpenEdge

OpenEdge is a kinetic Monte Carlo code for the edge of magnetically confined plasmas and for plasma-material interaction. It follows test particles, meaning neutral atoms and molecules, impurity ions, and solid or liquid particulates, through a plasma and magnetic-field background that is prescribed rather than solved for. The background comes from an edge fluid code such as SOLPS-ITER, SOLEDGE3X, OEDGE or EMC3-EIRENE, or from analytic or constant fields for verification.

Charged particles are advanced with a Boris integrator or, where the gyro-orbit is small compared with the field scale, a guiding-center integrator, with an automatic hand-off between the two. Atomic processes use ADAS rate coefficients. Particles feel Coulomb drag, thermal forces, cross-field diffusion and the wall sheath. At the wall they are reflected, sputter material, recycle, or are retained, and the surface composition evolves in response. Dust grains and droplets carry their own size, mass, charge and temperature and are heated, charged, dragged and evaporated by the plasma.

OpenEdge is built on SPARTA, the Direct Simulation Monte Carlo code from Sandia National Laboratories, and inherits its hierarchical Cartesian grid, cut-cell surface representation, particle storage, MPI parallelism and Kokkos performance-portability layer. Every SPARTA input command remains available. OpenEdge adds the physics commands documented in this manual.

OpenEdge runs on single-processor laptops and on parallel machines that compile C++17 and support MPI. A Kokkos build runs on multi-core CPUs and NVIDIA GPUs.

1.2 OpenEdge features

This section highlights OpenEdge features, with links to specific commands. The SPARTA features that OpenEdge inherits, such as the grid, surface, output and load-balancing commands, are documented on their own command pages.

Plasma backgrounds

  • read a converted plasma file with electron and ion density and temperature, parallel flow and equilibrium, fix background
  • native three-dimensional EMC3-EIRENE zones as well as two-dimensional axisymmetric meshes
  • constant and analytic backgrounds for verification
  • per-particle sampling of plasma quantities, magnetic field and gradients, compute plasma/fields

Charged-particle transport

  • Boris pusher with subcycling, global pusher
  • guiding-center pusher with RK2 or RK4 integration and a hybrid Boris/GCA hand-off near walls
  • sheath models: energy kick, spatial potential, and boundary barrier
  • Coulomb drag against the background plasma, fix coulomb/background, and binary Coulomb collisions between kinetic species, fix coulomb/binary
  • Braginskii thermal forces, fix force/thermal
  • anomalous cross-field diffusion and pinch, fix cross_field_diffusion
  • prescribed per-particle electric and magnetic fields, fix efield/particle, fix bfield/particle
  • absorption or reflection at a flux surface, fix reflect/psi

Atomic processes

  • ADAS ionization, recombination, charge exchange and dissociation with competing-channel selection, fix volume/chem/adas
  • neutral collisions against the background plasma, fix neutral/collide/background
  • synthetic line emission from photon-emission coefficients, compute volume/emissivity/grid

Plasma-wall interaction

  • wall-incident flux, impact energy and angle, sputter yield and gross erosion from tabulated or analytic yields, compute surface/physical/sputter
  • reflection, absorption, re-emission and sputtering with areal-density ledgers and strata for evolving wall composition, surf_react surface/pwi
  • neutral sources from recycling, sputtering and gas puffs, fix surface/emit/recycle, fix surface/emit/source, fix surface/emit/puff
  • liquid-metal surfaces with evaporation and adatom desorption, fix surface/state/lm

Particulates

  • dust grains and droplets with radius, mass and temperature state, material
  • injection from surfaces, fix particulate/emit
  • OML charging with thermionic and secondary-electron emission, fix particulate/charge
  • ion, neutral, electric and gravitational forces, fix particulate/drag, fix force/gravity
  • heating, phase change, evaporation and breakup, fix particulate/thermal
  • sticking or bouncing at walls, surf_collide particulate/bounce

Diagnostics and weighting

  • per-particle statistical weights and weighted grid moments, fix particle/weight, compute grid/weighted
  • distance and index of the nearest wall element per cell, compute nearest_surf/grid
  • all SPARTA dump, statistics and averaging commands

1.3 Grids, surfaces and plasma backgrounds

OpenEdge uses the SPARTA hierarchical Cartesian grid to store and sort particles, in 3d, 2d or 2d axisymmetric geometry. The vessel wall and any internal components are triangulated surfaces, or line segments in 2d, that cut through grid cells. Particles collide with these surfaces and the surface models decide what happens.

The plasma background is separate from the particle grid. It lives on the native mesh of the edge code it came from: a quadrilateral B2 mesh and its EIRENE triangulation for SOLPS, a triangulated mesh for SOLEDGE3X, or curvilinear zones for EMC3-EIRENE. Each particle samples the background at its own position through fix background, so the particle grid can be as coarse or as refined as the transport problem needs without touching the plasma data.

The magnetic field comes either from the poloidal flux map of the equilibrium, which gives the field and its derivatives analytically, or from field components stored with the background. A separate processes.h5 database supplies atomic rates and surface-interaction data.

1.4 Open source distribution

OpenEdge is distributed under the terms of the GNU General Public License version 2, the license of the SPARTA code it is built on. The LICENSE and LICENSE.SPARTA files in the distribution give the full text and the attribution of the SPARTA components. The NOTICES file lists third-party data and code included with OpenEdge and their licenses.

The GPL means you can use, modify and redistribute OpenEdge, and that any distributed derivative must also be released under the GPL. If you improve OpenEdge, please contribute the changes back through the repository so that others benefit.

1.5 Acknowledgments and citations

OpenEdge is developed at Oak Ridge National Laboratory. It would not exist without SPARTA, developed by Steve Plimpton and Michael Gallis at Sandia National Laboratories. The OpenEdge documentation reuses the SPARTA manual for the commands that OpenEdge inherits.

Atomic data come from the OPEN-ADAS project. Sputter yield and reflection tables are generated with the RustBCA binary collision code. Plasma backgrounds are produced with SOLPS-ITER, SOLEDGE3X, OEDGE and EMC3-EIRENE.
